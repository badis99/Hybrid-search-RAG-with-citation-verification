# FIFA Women's World Cup

The FIFA Women's World Cup is the international championship for senior women's national football teams. The first tournament was held in China in 1991 and, like the men's competition, it has been contested every four years since.

The United States is the most successful team in the competition's history, with four titles, won in 1991, 1999, 2015, and 2019. Germany has won twice, in 2003 and 2007, while Norway, Japan, and Spain have each won once. The 2011 title won by Japan made it the first Asian nation to become world champions in either the women's or men's game.

The 1999 final, held in the United States, is one of the most famous matches in the sport. The hosts beat China on penalties before a crowd of over 90,000 at the Rose Bowl in California, and the tournament is credited with dramatically raising the profile of women's football.

The most recent champions are Spain, who won the 2023 tournament co-hosted by Australia and New Zealand, beating England 1–0 in the final. The victory came shortly after the men's team's success and made Spain one of the few nations to hold both the men's and women's world titles within a few years of each other.
