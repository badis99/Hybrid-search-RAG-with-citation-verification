# 2002 World Cup: Brazil's fifth title

The 2002 World Cup was co-hosted by South Korea and Japan, the first World Cup held in Asia and the first shared between two nations. Brazil won the tournament, claiming a record fifth world title.

Brazil won all seven of their matches. Their attack was led by Ronaldo, who returned to form after his injury-hit years and finished as the tournament's top scorer with eight goals. He was supported by Rivaldo and a young Ronaldinho, an attacking trio often nicknamed the "three Rs".

The final was played against Germany at the International Stadium in Yokohama on 30 June 2002, the first competitive meeting between the two nations at a World Cup. Brazil won 2–0, with Ronaldo scoring both goals. His performance was seen as a personal redemption after the disappointment of the 1998 final.

The tournament was also remembered for several major upsets, including co-hosts South Korea reaching the semi-finals and the elimination of France, the defending champions, without scoring a goal in the group stage. Germany's goalkeeper Oliver Kahn was named the tournament's best player, the only goalkeeper to win the award.
