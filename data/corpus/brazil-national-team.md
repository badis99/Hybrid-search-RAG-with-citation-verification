# Brazil national football team

Brazil is the most successful team in the history of the FIFA World Cup, with five titles won in 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, and 2002. It is also the only national team to have qualified for and appeared at every World Cup finals since the tournament began in 1930.

Brazil's playing style, often called "jogo bonito" or the beautiful game, is associated with attacking flair and individual skill. The team plays in a distinctive yellow shirt with green trim and blue shorts, a kit adopted after the 1950 defeat prompted a redesign of the previous white strip.

The nation has produced many of football's greatest players. Pelé, the only man to win three World Cups, is the most celebrated; later stars include Garrincha, Zico, Romário, Ronaldo, Ronaldinho, and Neymar. Ronaldo was the World Cup's all-time leading scorer until his record was passed by Miroslav Klose in 2014.

Brazil's first title came in 1958 in Sweden, when a seventeen-year-old Pelé announced himself on the world stage. Their most admired side was the 1970 team, while their most painful memories are the 1950 Maracanazo and the 7–1 semi-final defeat to Germany in 2014.
