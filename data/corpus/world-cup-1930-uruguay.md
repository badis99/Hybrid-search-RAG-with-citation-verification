# 1930 World Cup: the first tournament

The inaugural FIFA World Cup was held in Uruguay from 13 to 30 July 1930. Uruguay was chosen as host to mark the centenary of its first constitution and because the country were the reigning Olympic football champions, having won the gold medal in 1924 and 1928. As host, Uruguay also paid the travel expenses of the visiting teams.

Thirteen teams took part: seven from South America, four from Europe, and two from North America. There was no qualification tournament; entry was by invitation. Only four European sides made the long sea voyage across the Atlantic, a source of lasting tension between the continents.

The matches were played in three stadiums in Montevideo, all built for the occasion. The largest was the Estadio Centenario, which became the centrepiece of the tournament. The first two World Cup matches took place simultaneously on 13 July, with France beating Mexico 4–1 and the United States defeating Belgium 3–0. Lucien Laurent of France scored the very first goal in World Cup history.

Uruguay and Argentina reached the final, contested at the Estadio Centenario on 30 July before a crowd of roughly 68,000. Uruguay came from behind to win 4–2, becoming the first world champions. The victory set off national celebrations, and the following day was declared a public holiday in Uruguay.
