# The World Cup trophies

Two different trophies have been awarded to World Cup winners. From 1930 to 1970 the prize was the Jules Rimet Trophy, originally called "Victory" but renamed in 1946 to honour the FIFA president who launched the tournament. It depicted Nike, the Greek goddess of victory, holding an octagonal cup.

Under the original rules, a nation that won the tournament three times would keep the Jules Rimet Trophy permanently. Brazil achieved this with its third title in 1970 and took the trophy home for good. That original trophy was stolen in Rio de Janeiro in 1983 and has never been recovered; it is believed to have been melted down.

For the 1974 tournament FIFA commissioned a new prize, the FIFA World Cup Trophy, designed by the Italian sculptor Silvio Gazzaniga. It stands 36.8 centimetres tall, weighs 6.1 kilograms, and is made of 18-carat gold with two bands of malachite around the base. The design shows two human figures holding up the Earth.

Unlike its predecessor, the current trophy is not given away permanently. The winning nation receives the original only during the celebrations immediately after the final and is presented with a gold-plated replica to keep. The names of the winning countries are engraved on the underside of the base.
