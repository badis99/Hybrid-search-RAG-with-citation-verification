# 1986 World Cup: Maradona's tournament

The 1986 World Cup was held in Mexico, which stepped in as host after Colombia withdrew for economic reasons. It is remembered above all for the performances of Diego Maradona, who is widely considered to have dominated a single World Cup more completely than any other player.

The defining match was the quarter-final between Argentina and England on 22 June 1986 at the Estadio Azteca. Within four minutes of each other, Maradona scored two of the most famous goals in the sport's history. The first, the "Hand of God", was punched into the net with his hand, unseen by the officials. The second, the "Goal of the Century", saw him dribble past five England players from inside his own half before scoring.

Argentina reached the final against West Germany. Argentina led 2–0, were pegged back to 2–2, and won 3–1 when Jorge Burruchaga scored the decisive goal after a pass from Maradona. It was Argentina's second world title, following their win as hosts in 1978.

Maradona captained the side, scored five goals, provided five assists, and was named the tournament's best player. His individual influence over the 1986 campaign is often held up as the greatest by any footballer at a single World Cup.
