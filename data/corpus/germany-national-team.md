# Germany national football team

Germany is one of the most successful national teams in international football, with four World Cup titles won in 1954, 1974, 1990, and 2014. The first three were won as West Germany, before reunification; the 2014 title was the first as a single unified Germany. Germany has also reached the World Cup final on eight occasions, more than any other nation.

The team's first title, in 1954, is known as the "Miracle of Bern". West Germany came from two goals down to beat the heavily favoured Hungary side of Ferenc Puskás 3–2 in the final in Switzerland, an upset credited with lifting national morale in the post-war years.

Germany is known for tactical discipline, organisation, and reliability in tournament football, qualities sometimes summarised in the saying that football is a simple game in which the Germans always win. Great German players include Franz Beckenbauer, Gerd Müller, Lothar Matthäus, and Miroslav Klose.

Beckenbauer won the World Cup as captain in 1974 and as manager in 1990, one of only a handful of people to lift the trophy as both player and coach. Klose, with sixteen goals across four tournaments, is the World Cup's all-time leading scorer.
