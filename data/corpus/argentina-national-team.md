# Argentina national football team

Argentina has won the FIFA World Cup three times, in 1978, 1986, and 2022. The team plays in distinctive light blue and white striped shirts, the colours of the national flag, and is one of the strongest footballing nations in South America alongside its great rival Brazil.

Argentina's first title came as hosts in 1978, when they beat the Netherlands 3–1 after extra time in the final in Buenos Aires. The striker Mario Kempes was the tournament's top scorer and its outstanding player. The tournament took place under the country's military dictatorship, which has coloured its legacy.

The 1986 title was driven by Diego Maradona, whose performances, including the "Hand of God" and the "Goal of the Century" against England, are among the most famous in the sport. Maradona became a national hero, and his death in 2020 was marked by three days of national mourning.

Argentina's third title came in 2022 in Qatar, when Lionel Messi finally lifted the trophy after defeat in the 2014 final and in earlier finals of the Copa América. Between 2021 and 2022 the team won the Copa América, the World Cup, and the Finalissima against Italy, a run that cemented its place among the greatest sides of the era.
