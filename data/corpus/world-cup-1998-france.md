# 1998 World Cup: France's first title

The 1998 World Cup was hosted by France and was the first to feature 32 teams, expanded from the 24 of previous editions. France won the tournament, claiming its first world title on home soil.

France reached the final against the defending champions Brazil, played at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis on 12 July 1998. France won 3–0. Zinedine Zidane scored twice with headers in the first half, and Emmanuel Petit added a third in stoppage time. The victory sparked celebrations across the country, with vast crowds gathering on the Champs-Élysées in Paris.

The final was overshadowed beforehand by uncertainty over Brazil's Ronaldo, who suffered a convulsive fit hours before kick-off. He was initially left out of the starting line-up, then reinstated, and played well below his best. The full circumstances have been debated ever since.

France's team, coached by Aimé Jacquet and built around Zidane, was celebrated as a symbol of a diverse, multicultural nation. France would go on to reach the final again in 2006 and win a second title in 2018.
