# The FIFA World Cup

The FIFA World Cup is the premier international association football tournament, contested by the senior men's national teams of the members of FIFA, the sport's global governing body. It has been awarded every four years since the first tournament in 1930, except in 1942 and 1946, when it was not held because of the Second World War.

The competition has two parts: a qualification phase, which takes place over the preceding three years to decide which teams reach the tournament, and the final tournament, which is hosted by one or more countries over roughly one month. The 1930 edition featured 13 teams; the field grew to 24 in 1982, to 32 in 1998, and will expand to 48 teams beginning with the 2026 edition co-hosted by Canada, Mexico, and the United States.

Eight national teams have won the trophy. Brazil is the most successful, with five titles, and is the only nation to have appeared at every World Cup finals. Germany and Italy have four titles each, Argentina has three, Uruguay and France have two each, and England and Spain have one apiece. All winners have come from either Europe or South America.

The tournament is one of the most widely viewed sporting events in the world. It is a showcase for the sport's greatest players and has produced many of football's defining moments, from Pelé's emergence in 1958 to Diego Maradona's performances in 1986 and Lionel Messi's title with Argentina in 2022.
