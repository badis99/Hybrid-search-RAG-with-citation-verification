# 1966 World Cup: England's title

The 1966 World Cup was hosted by England, the birthplace of modern football, and remains the only time the English men's national team has won the tournament. The competition was held from 11 to 30 July 1966, with the final staged at Wembley Stadium in London.

England, managed by Alf Ramsey and captained by Bobby Moore, reached the final against West Germany. The match finished 2–2 after ninety minutes and went to extra time. Geoff Hurst scored twice in extra time to complete his hat-trick, and England won 4–2. Hurst remains the only player to have scored a hat-trick in a men's World Cup final.

Hurst's second goal of the match, England's third, is one of the most debated in the sport's history. His shot struck the underside of the crossbar and bounced down near the goal line; the Soviet linesman Tofiq Bahramov signalled that the ball had crossed the line, and the goal was given. Replays have never settled the argument definitively.

The tournament also saw the first World Cup mascot, a lion named World Cup Willie, and the emergence of Portugal's Eusébio, who finished as the tournament's top scorer with nine goals. Bobby Moore lifting the trophy on the Wembley pitch became one of the enduring images of English sport.
