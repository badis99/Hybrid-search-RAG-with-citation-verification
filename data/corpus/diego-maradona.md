# Diego Maradona

Diego Armando Maradona was an Argentine attacking midfielder and forward, widely regarded, alongside Pelé, as one of the two greatest footballers of the twentieth century. Born in 1960 near Buenos Aires, he was known for his close control, low centre of gravity, and left-footed skill.

Maradona's defining achievement was the 1986 World Cup in Mexico, which Argentina won and which he is considered to have dominated more than any player has dominated a single tournament. In the quarter-final against England he scored the "Hand of God" goal, punched in illegally, and minutes later the "Goal of the Century", a solo run past five defenders.

At club level his most celebrated years were with Napoli in Italy, whom he led to their only two Serie A league titles, in 1987 and 1990, turning a modest club into champions of Italy. He remains a revered figure in the city of Naples.

Maradona's career and later life were marked by struggles with cocaine addiction and ill health. He was expelled from the 1994 World Cup after failing a drug test. He later worked as a coach, including a spell managing the Argentina national team at the 2010 World Cup. He died in November 2020, prompting three days of national mourning in Argentina.
