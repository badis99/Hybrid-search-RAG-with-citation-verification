# 1950 World Cup: the Maracanazo

The 1950 World Cup in Brazil was the first held after the Second World War, the previous two editions having been cancelled. It remains the only World Cup decided not by a single-match final but by a final round-robin group of four teams: Uruguay, Brazil, Sweden, and Spain.

Brazil went into the deciding match against Uruguay on 16 July 1950 needing only a draw to be crowned champions. The game was played at the newly built Maracanã stadium in Rio de Janeiro before a crowd officially recorded at just under 200,000, still among the largest ever to watch a football match.

Brazil took the lead early in the second half through Friaça. Uruguay equalised through Juan Alberto Schiaffino and then, with about eleven minutes remaining, Alcides Ghiggia scored the winner to make it 2–1. Uruguay had won the World Cup for the second time.

The defeat stunned the host nation and became known as the "Maracanazo", or the Maracanã blow. It is remembered in Brazil as a national tragedy, and the goalkeeper Moacyr Barbosa was unfairly blamed for the loss for the rest of his life. The result is one of the most famous upsets in the history of the sport.
