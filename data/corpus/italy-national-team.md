# Italy national football team

Italy has won the FIFA World Cup four times, in 1934, 1938, 1982, and 2006, a total second only to Brazil. The team is known by the nickname "Gli Azzurri", the Blues, after the azure blue of its shirts, a colour drawn from the royal house of Savoy.

Italy's first two titles came in the 1930s under the coach Vittorio Pozzo, the only manager to win the World Cup twice. The 1934 tournament was hosted and won by Italy, and the team retained the trophy in France in 1938.

The 1982 title in Spain was inspired by the striker Paolo Rossi, who returned from a two-year suspension to finish as the tournament's top scorer with six goals, including a hat-trick against Brazil and both goals in the semi-final. Italy beat West Germany 3–1 in the final.

Italy's fourth title came in 2006 in Germany. The final against France ended 1–1 and was decided on penalties, which Italy won 5–3. The match is also remembered for Zinedine Zidane being sent off in extra time for headbutting Marco Materazzi in the chest, the last act of Zidane's playing career. Italian football is traditionally associated with the defensive system known as "catenaccio".
