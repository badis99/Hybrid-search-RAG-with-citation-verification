# 1970 World Cup: Brazil's third title

The 1970 World Cup, held in Mexico, is often regarded as one of the finest tournaments in the competition's history and produced what many consider the greatest team ever to play the game. It was the first World Cup broadcast in colour and the first held in North America.

Brazil won all six of their matches. Their side included Pelé, Jairzinho, Gérson, Tostão, Rivelino, and the captain Carlos Alberto. Jairzinho scored in every round, the only player to do so in a single tournament. Brazil met Italy in the final at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City on 21 June 1970.

Brazil won the final 4–1. Pelé opened the scoring with a header, and the match was sealed by a celebrated team goal finished by Carlos Alberto, a flowing move involving most of the outfield players that is frequently cited as the greatest goal in World Cup history. It was Pelé's third World Cup winner's medal.

Because this was Brazil's third title, the nation was awarded the Jules Rimet Trophy permanently under the rules of the time. The 1970 team remains a benchmark against which attacking football is measured.
